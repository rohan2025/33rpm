A record player on your Mac desktop.

33rpm puts a turntable on your wallpaper that plays along with YouTube Music. The record spins at 33⅓, the arm follows the song, and every track you play ends up in the crate.

The details

It behaves like the real thing.

Nothing to open or arrange. Play a song in YouTube Music and the room responds.

The record spins at 33⅓

Album art on the label, the arm creeping inward as the song plays, and an easy slow-down to a stop when you pause.

A stereo that shows the song

The title and artist glow on an amber dot-matrix display. Its knobs go back, pause and skip.

Every song ends up in the crate

When a song ends its sleeve drops into the crate. Hover to flip through, click one to play it again.

Three rooms

Pick the mood.

Switch rooms from the menu bar. Each one is just a folder of images, so you can make your own.

Evening

Lamp light, dust drifting in the glow, the city outside.

Rainy Window

Blue dusk and rain running down the glass.

Minimal

Calm and clean, for a desktop that stays out of the way.

Setup

Two minutes, three steps.

Install the app

Download 33rpm, open the file and drag 33rpm into Applications.

Add the extension

Add 33rpm Bridge to Chrome. It tells the app what YouTube Music is playing.

Pair and play

Copy the pairing code from 33rpm's Setup Guide, paste it into the extension, and play something on music.youtube.com.

The first time you open it, macOS will say it can't verify the developer, because 33rpm is a free hobby app that isn't notarized by Apple. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.

Privacy

Everything stays on your Mac.

The extension talks only to the app on your own computer. Nothing goes to a server, because there isn't one.
